# Optional second strikes (Amateur and Master)

After a non-tied contest removes the losing army's final card or formation, pause before replenishment and offer the victorious side a second strike against that side's exposed heir. This applies to a successful attacker or defender, humans or computer opponents, in solo and multiplayer games.

The offer explicitly warns that losing eliminates the challenger's entire side, even if it still has its heir and other army cards. Accepting permits any normally eligible card or formation and a new eligible statistic. The heir restriction on initiating units still applies. The second strike may target only the heir exposed by the first contest.

- Decline: complete the original victory's normal replenishment and turn sequence.
- Win: eliminate the opposing side. Standard play ends immediately; long play continues until only one side remains.
- Lose: eliminate the entire challenging side. The defending side wins in standard play; long play uses its normal elimination rule.
- Tie or cancellation: eliminate neither side, end the attempt, and resume the original victory's normal replenishment and turn sequence.

There is at most one second strike per qualifying contest. It does not advance the game round or reset the normal turn order until that contest and its replenishment are complete. A second strike cannot generate another second strike.

Nile Flood dice are rolled afresh. Master comparison effects, immunities, interrupts, and guarantees retain their normal precedence. Accepting a second strike requires declaring that comparison before using effects; it does not grant a separate army-effect turn. A comparison cancelled by an interrupt is not a loss. A clock reversal restores its earlier turn normally.

Computer opponents only accept when a revealed eligible unit has a winning printed-stat margin over the exposed heir (more than five with Floods enabled). Hidden cards are excluded from this decision. Special comparison effects can still change the result.

Multiplayer decisions are stored in the existing room state. Only the victorious human may answer their offer; computer choices are made by the server. Existing authentication, room membership, revision checks, and hidden-card filtering continue to apply. No database migration is needed.
